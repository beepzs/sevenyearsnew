How do I help my child’s gut recover after antibiotics?

E
Dr. Elisa SongGuest · Episode 26 · Integrative pediatrician
Published 9 Sep 2026 Reviewed 22 Sep 2026 Pending clinical review
Reviewed by Dr. Murray Clarke, clinical lead · scientific reviewer to be named
How we review

Every answer goes through scientific review — and legal and regulatory review where needed — before it is published. Each page shows when it was published and last reviewed, and high-traffic pages are re-checked every quarter. Our editorial standards

The short answer

First, antibiotics are sometimes essential — always finish the course you were prescribed. Afterwards, a varied, fibre-rich diet, fermented foods if tolerated, plenty of water and time all help the microbiome rebuild. Ask your pediatrician whether a probiotic is worth adding for your child.

Medical disclaimer

This answer is general education from a named expert, reviewed before publishing. It is not medical advice, a diagnosis or a treatment plan for your child, and reading it does not create a doctor–patient relationship. Always check with your child’s own doctor before changing their care, diet or supplements. Full medical disclaimer

Get urgent help now — call 911 or your local emergency number — if your child:

  • is struggling to breathe, or has blue or grey lips
  • is very drowsy, floppy or hard to wake
  • has a seizure
  • has swelling of the face or lips, or hives with breathing trouble
  • is under 3 months old with a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher

Watch the full answer

Dr. Elisa Song answers this in the video above, a 9-minute video from The First Seven Years. For the whole discussion, watch the full episode.

When to talk to your doctor

If you are worried about your child, if symptoms are getting worse, or if something just doesn’t feel right, speak to your pediatrician. Trust your instincts — you know your child best.
